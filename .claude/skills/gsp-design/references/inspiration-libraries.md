# Design inspiration libraries, full guide

## Real, shipped product patterns, the highest value tier for dashboard or flow work
**Mobbin**: the largest searchable library of real app and web screens and flows, hundreds of thousands of screens across roughly 1,400 apps, with a paid tier. Best for benchmarking one specific pattern (an empty state, an upgrade flow, an onboarding sequence) against what real shipped products actually do, including well known apps like Duolingo and Airbnb. Has a Figma plugin. Limitation, mostly static screens rather than motion.
**SaaSUI** (saasui.design): free, curated real SaaS screens organised by more than 20 screen and pattern types, dashboards, onboarding, pricing, settings, empty states, across many verticals including AI. Best when the task is a specific screen type and you want it shown in the context of the rest of the product.
**SaaSFrame**: thousands of screenshots spanning product, marketing, and email sequences. Best when a task crosses product and marketing in the same flow, a landing page into a pricing page into an onboarding email into a dashboard.
**Page Flows**: paid, video recordings of complete real user journeys. Best for understanding motion and state changes in an onboarding or activation flow, not just static screens.
**SaaS Interface** (saasinterface.com): a gallery focused specifically on SaaS dashboards. Good for comparing card layouts, widget composition, and sidebar patterns side by side.

## Award level visual and motion craft
**Awwwards**: Site of the Day, Month, and Year, plus nominees, organised by trending category (UI design, animation, scrolling), by industry category, and by technology (WebGL, GSAP, Framer, React, Figma). Strong for high craft art direction and motion, but many winners are marketing or experiential sites rather than product UI, judge accordingly. A practitioner note worth keeping in mind: award winning sites have to simultaneously nail art direction, motion, and real device performance, a heavy 3D hero that runs at 18fps on a mid range phone loses regardless of how it looks in the case study video.
**Godly**: a much smaller, more tightly curated feed, two or three new sites a week, favouring restrained, editorial, typographically considered work over novelty. The better source when the goal is quality over volume.
**SiteInspire**: running since 2010, clean and editorial, with strong filtering by style, type, subject, and CMS.

## Marketing and landing pages specifically
**Saaspo**: curated SaaS websites organised by industry and by page section. **Land-book**: curated landing pages and websites, filterable down to a specific section like a pricing table or a comparison grid. **Lapa Ninja**: a large landing page library sorted by industry, including an education category. **Landingfolio**: similar curated landing page collection.

## Raw component and micro interaction patterns
**Collect UI**: over 14,000 references built out of the Daily UI challenge and the Dribbble ecosystem, organised by pattern, free. **UI Movement**: curated interaction and animation patterns, good for micro interaction ideas specifically. **UI Garage**: another pattern focused collection.

## Concept and portfolio galleries, useful for direction, not shipped truth
**Dribbble**: idealised concepts and portfolio pieces. Genuinely useful for early visual direction and spotting emerging aesthetic trends, but shots on Dribbble are frequently optimised for likes and portfolio appeal over usability, and the AI product design corner of Dribbble specifically has converged on a recognisable, slightly generic look, see `ai-feature-patterns.md` for what that look is and why to avoid copying it directly. Always sanity check a Dribbble sourced idea against a real shipped product on Mobbin or Page Flows before committing to it.
**Behance**: longer form case studies with actual design rationale attached, useful for understanding the thinking behind a direction, not just the final shot.

## Typography and branding specific
**Typewolf** and **Fonts in Use** for typography direction. **Brand New** and **Identity Designed** for branding case studies. **Motionographer** for motion and animation specifically, complementary to the animation-library skill's own research.

## How to actually use this list day to day
Start broad for direction (Pinterest, Awwwards, Behance), then get specific for the exact thing being built (Mobbin for mobile or product flows, SaaSUI or Saaspo for SaaS, Screenlane or Collect UI for an individual pattern). Don't rely on a single source for any real decision, mixing three to five regularly avoids design tunnel vision, and cross checking a concept sourced from Dribbble or Awwwards against a real shipped product on Mobbin or Page Flows catches the gap between what looks good in a shot and what actually works in use.
